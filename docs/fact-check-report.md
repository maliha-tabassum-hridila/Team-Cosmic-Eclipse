## Specific findings and corrections

- **Sojourner:** NASA’s Photojournal record says the final Pathfinder data transmission arrived at 10:23 UTC on Sept. 27, 1997; the successful mission was terminated on Mar. 10, 1998. The Explore panel’s Sept. 27 data-session wording should not be interpreted as formal mission termination.
- **Pioneer 11:** NASA Science lists launch as Apr. 6, 1973 / 02:11 UT. It separately lists last contact (Sept. 30, 1995) and last engineering data received (Nov. 24, 1995). The UTC/UT date is used rather than a potentially ambiguous local calendar date.
- **Voyagers:** NASA’s current instrument-status page was reviewed. It states Voyager 1 is expected to reach one light-day on Nov. 18, 2026; that is a forecast at this report date. Both spacecraft are outside the heliosphere, but “interstellar space” should not be conflated with having left the Solar System in every possible boundary definition.
- **Surveyor 7 laser claim:** NASA’s page confirms it was used as a target for Earth-based lasers to measure the Earth–Moon distance. Do not describe it as carrying an onboard laser instrument. NASA lists Feb. 21, 1968 as end of mission; communications paused Jan. 26 and resumed Feb. 12.
- **Apollo packages:** the blanket claim that all six descent stages remain is not enough to establish which scientific experiment packages remain at each site. The current report retains uncertainty and does not claim a full package-by-package inventory.
- **Explore source links:** Sojourner’s hotspot claims can use the direct NASA Photojournal record above. InSight, Surveyor 1, and Surveyor 5 hotspot-specific assertions still require a line-by-line check against their linked mission pages. General mission landing pages alone may not support every detailed hotspot statement.
